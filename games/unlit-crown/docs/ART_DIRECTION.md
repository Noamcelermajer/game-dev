# The Unlit Crown: Art Direction

## The approach

**The game has no drawn art assets.** Everything is simple 3D, drawn at a very low resolution and turned into pixel art by the renderer. Light and shadow are real and update every frame.

- **The world** (arenas, walls, floors, props) is generated by code in the game.
- **Characters and bosses** are low-detail models built in Blender by scripts in `art/`, with a small rig and animations, and exported as `.glb` files. The game replaces their materials with its own, so they are lit and outlined like everything else.

AI-generated images are used only as **mood boards and comparison targets**. The official style anchor is [concept/00-style-anchor.webp](concept/00-style-anchor.webp): every scene the game renders is judged side by side against a target image like it.

## The locked look

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Game resolution | **384×216**, scaled by whole numbers (×5 = 1080p, ×10 = 4K) |
| World scale | 16 pixels per world unit |
| Camera | Fixed, high three-quarter view (about 32° above the ground), no rotation |
| Hero | about 20–24 px tall, strong silhouette, 1 px dark outline, the lantern as the bright focal point |
| Bosses | 80–190 px (4–8× the hero), built from simple shapes and animated by moving their parts |
| Palette | A fixed set of about 32–40 colours per region, sampled from that region's target image |
| Light falloff | Hard steps, which come from snapping to the palette |
| Outlines | 1 px dark outline on every solid object, light top edges, dark inner corners |
| Saturated colour | Reserved for light, magic and danger |
| Composition | Dark edges, a lit centre, tiny hero, huge bosses |

## How a frame is made

1. **Scene:** chunky low-poly geometry (stacked blocks, columns) generated by code from a seed. Wall heights are whole brick courses so tops line up with mortar lines.
2. **Surface detail:** stone patterns are drawn by the material in screen pixels, so one texel is exactly one pixel: coursed bricks on walls, ringed flagstones on the floor, each with a lit top edge and a shaded bottom edge.
3. **Lighting:** real point lights with hard-edged shadows (lantern, braziers, crown shard), plus a faint violet ambient so shadows are never pure black.
4. **Low-res render:** the lit scene is drawn to a 384×216 image with no smoothing. A second pass records the depth and facing of every solid surface.
5. **Pixel-art pass:**
   - **Outlines:** a dark line wherever one object stands in front of another.
   - **Edge highlights:** a light line along ridges, such as the top front edge of a block.
   - **Corner shading:** a dark line in inner corners, such as where a wall meets the floor.
   - **Palette:** every pixel is snapped to the nearest of 38 fixed colours. 32 were sampled from the style anchor; 6 are accents for bone, flame, gold and the crown.
   - **Vignette:** the edges of the screen darken.
6. **Upscale:** the image is scaled by a whole number with sharp pixels.

Effects (flames, the crown, sparks, glow) and tiny details (bones, candles, grass) are drawn without outlines, so they stay bright and readable. Flames are drawn on the pixel grid by a shader and animate at about 12 frames per second to feel hand-animated.

## Light is measurable

Because the lights are real, the game can work out how lit any spot is, with walls and pillars blocking light. The Light and Dread systems, the Gloamwalker's shadow bonuses and boss blackout phases all read from this one value.

## Rules for character models

- Very low detail (the hero is under 500 triangles) and exaggerated proportions: big hood, big lantern.
- Flat colours only, one material per colour. Glowing parts use a material whose name starts with `glow`.
- The model faces -Y in Blender and is built from rigid parts parented to bones.
- Parts thinner than about 2 px on screen (sword, lantern frame) are drawn without outlines, or they turn black.
- Animations are exported smooth and played back in steps of 12 frames per second.

## Known limits and rules

- **Shadow-casting lights:** at most 4–6 per room. Candles and embers glow without casting shadows.
- **Camera movement:** the camera moves only in whole-pixel steps to stop pixels shimmering.
- **Walls in front of the player** are kept low or faded so they never hide the hero.
- **Desktop browsers first.** Phones get a reduced mode later.

## Approval checklist for any scene

- Readable at 1× size: the hero's silhouette is clear in the dark.
- Saturated colour appears only on light, magic or danger.
- Light steps are visible on the floor around each light.
- Every stone and object has a clear outline and a lit top edge.
- A screenshot holds up next to its target image.
